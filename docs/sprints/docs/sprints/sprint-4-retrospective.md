Sprint 4 – Retrospective
What Went Well ✅

Final MVP polish improved clarity and usability
Locking the category/tag system to seven consistent categories made the homepage more readable and solved visibility issues where some tags did not appear reliably.

UI refresh enhanced visual quality
New fonts and color palette significantly improved the overall look of the product, making the MVP feel more polished and closer to a production-ready app.

Deployment remained stable throughout the sprint
No outages or major regressions occurred during UI updates or category changes, demonstrating improvement in our deployment pipeline and environment stability.

What Didn’t Go Well ⚠️

Mandatory technical requirements were delayed
The A/B test endpoint and analytics tracking were underestimated and deferred despite being required for the final submission.

Sprint scope prioritized polish over compliance tasks
While UI improvements added user value, equal priority should have been placed on final infrastructure and testing requirements earlier in the sprint.

What We Will Improve 💡

Mandatory features will be prioritized first moving forward
Action items tied to grading requirements will always precede UI optimizations in sprints and final-task planning.

→ Action: Backlog priority re-rank to prioritize all compliance-driven tasks first.

Daily sprint checkpoints will be added
Short check-ins will confirm progress toward major requirements to avoid late discoveries of unfinished work.

→ Action: Add 10-minute daily standups through final submission period.

Team Dynamics Reflection

Team collaboration improved steadily across the semester:

Sprint 2 focused on tool adoption and workflow stabilization.

Sprint 3 established reliable team rhythms with cleaner PR practices and balanced task ownership.

Sprint 4 demonstrated strong coordination during UI polish but showed the need for sharper prioritization discipline around grading-critical deliverables.

Overall, communication remained open and respectful, and peer feedback in pull requests became a meaningful learning channel for all members.

Sprint-to-Project Reflection

Across all sprints, the team learned:

Scope discipline is critical: MVP focus allowed us to deliver complete user journeys rather than scattered partial features.

Early CI and testing pay off: Automation and migration discipline prevented deployment disasters later.

Process matters as much as code: Planning documents, retrospectives, and GitHub tracking made workload distribution transparent and manageable.

User-focused features guide priorities: Keeping the “host → discover → RSVP” loop central helped guide decisions when cutting or deferring features.

Sprint 4 reinforced the importance of aligning sprint objectives with mandatory project requirements and identifying critical compliance tasks earlier in sprint planning.
